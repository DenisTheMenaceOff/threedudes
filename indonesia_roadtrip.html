Stepping into six centuries of empire

Red Fort (Lal Qila)

Mughal emperor Shah Jahan's primary residence from 1648. 2.5 km of red sandstone walls enclose palaces, baths, and a mosque. The Diwan-i-Khas hall once displayed the Peacock Throne. UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Best before 09:00 — half the crowd, better light
See photos

Humayun's Tomb

Built 1570 by Humayun's widow. The first garden-tomb of the Mughal tradition — it directly inspired the Taj Mahal built 60 years later. The symmetry, the scale, the quiet. UNESCO, and consistently uncrowded.

Walk the Char Bagh garden at golden hour

Taj Mahal

Built by Shah Jahan 1632–1653 in memory of Mumtaz Mahal. 22 years, 20,000 workers, 28 types of semi-precious stone inlaid into white Makrana marble. The minarets lean slightly outward — to fall away from the tomb if they collapse. Nothing prepares you.

Afternoon light turns marble orange-gold. Sunrise is also remarkable but requires 05:00 queues.
See photos

Fatehpur Sikri

Emperor Akbar built this city in red sandstone in 1571, then abandoned it 15 years later — likely for lack of water. A perfectly preserved ghost capital. The Buland Darwaza (Victory Gate) at 54 m is still the world's tallest mosque gateway. UNESCO.

Visit late afternoon — light on sandstone is intense red and photographers chase it
See photos

The Pink City

Amber Fort & Sheesh Mahal

Rajput fortress built 1592 on a ridge above Jaipur. The Sheesh Mahal (Hall of Mirrors) inside is the extraordinary moment: every surface covered in tiny mirror glass, a single candle flame multiplied into a sky of stars. Walk up — the elephant ride is optional and ethically contentious.

Come before 16:00 — afternoon light through the jali screens is magical
See photos

Hawa Mahal

The Palace of Winds — built 1799 by Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh as a screen from which royal women could observe street festivities. 953 small casement windows carved from pink sandstone. Five storeys, 15 m wide, only 30 cm deep in places. One of the most photographed facades in India.

Cross the street and find the rooftop café opposite — best angle for the full facade

Kota Tua (Old Batavia)

The old Dutch colonial city, established by VOC in 1619. Fatahillah Square surrounded by colonial-era buildings — now museums, cafés, and the Wayang puppet museum. Vendors rent vintage bicycles for riding the brick streets. Best at golden hour when the buildings glow.

Café Batavia (1805 building) for afternoon coffee overlooking the square
See photos

Monas — National Monument

132-metre white marble obelisk topped with a 35 kg flame coated in gold. Commissioned by Sukarno in 1961 as a symbol of Indonesia's independence. The underground museum traces 400 years of colonial history in 48 dioramas. The observation deck gives a 360° view of the capital.

Underground museum is the real gem — walk through Indonesian history chronologically
See photos
Bike tips for Java

Buy helmets in Jakarta (cheaper and better fit than renting). Mesh riding jackets with armour — mandatory for multi-hour riding in heat. Indonesian traffic rule: motorbikes keep left, filter through gaps. Fuel: Pertamina pumps every 30–50 km. Always top up at ¾ tank in rural Java. Riding in convoy: lead bike sets pace, last bike calls hazards.

Keraton Kasepuhan

One of the oldest surviving royal palaces in Java, founded 15th century by Sunan Gunung Jati. Unlike Javanese kratons, this mixes Hindu, Islamic, and Dutch colonial elements — evidence of Cirebon's position as a trading port. The museum holds antique gamelan instruments and the ceremonial royal carriage Singa Barong.

Visit late afternoon — fewer tourists, the guide speaks English
See photos

Gua Sunyaragi

A royal cave-garden retreat built in 1703 from coral and stone. Labyrinthine passages, hidden chambers, decorative grottos — built for the sultan's meditation and recreation. The architecture is unique to Cirebon: part Hindu temple, part Islamic garden, part Dutch waterwork. Often overlooked by tourists.

Best explored slowly — many passages have no signs and the surprise is the point
See photos

Lawang Sewu, Semarang

"Thousand Doors" — built 1907 as HQ for the Dutch colonial railway system. Vast Art Nouveau building with stained glass windows, tiled corridors, and underground tunnels used as prison cells during the Japanese occupation. The 1945 Battle of Semarang was fought in and around this building — it's as much a battlefield memorial as an architectural landmark.

Underground tour available — bring a light. Best photographed from the front gate.
See photos

Pagilaran Tea Estate

Colonial-era tea plantation in the mountains between Pekalongan and Semarang. At 800 m altitude, mornings bring mist rolling between the rows. You can walk into the terraces while pickers work around you — the contrast of red baskets and bright green rows is the image. Often completely empty of tourists.

Arrive before 09:00 for the mist. The plantation road itself is one of the best rides of the trip.
See photos

Borobudur at sunrise

9th century Mahayana Buddhist temple pyramid — 2 million blocks of volcanic stone, 504 Buddha statues, 2,672 carved relief panels. It is the largest Buddhist monument ever built. At dawn, Semeru volcano smokes in the background, mist fills the valleys below, and stupas emerge from the dark one by one. UNESCO. Nothing in Indonesia compares.

Leave at 04:00. Bring a headlamp. The sunrise ticket must be booked 2–3 days ahead online.
See photos

Prambanan

9th century Hindu temple compound dedicated to the Trimurti — Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva. Built by the Sanjaya dynasty just 50 years after Borobudur. The Shiva temple at 47 m is the tallest Hindu structure in Indonesia. The two great temple complexes — Buddhist and Hindu — built just 10 km apart, within decades of each other.

Sunset behind the spires — visit in the last 2 hours of the day for the best light
See photos

Kraton Surakarta, Solo

The royal court of the Sunanate of Surakarta — one of the two successor states of the Mataram Sultanate. The sultan's descendants still occupy the inner chambers. The museum wing displays the original gamelan orchestra instruments, gilded royal carriages, and ceremonial regalia. Solo is the more traditional, less touristed sibling of Yogyakarta.

Pasar Klewer next door: Indonesia's biggest batik market. Go in the morning for the full chaos.
See photos

Singosari Temple, Malang

13th century funerary temple of King Kertanegara — one of the finest surviving monuments of the Singosari kingdom. Built in East Javanese style with fine andesite carvings. Notably unfinished: the workers simply stopped one day in 1292 when the kingdom collapsed. The scaffolding marks still show on the upper blocks. Rarely visited. Completely free.

Combine with Coban Rondo waterfall (30 km away) for a half-day loop from Malang
See photos

Penanjakan Sunrise Viewpoint

The canonical image of Java: Semeru (3676 m, active) erupting in the far background, Bromo smoking in the middle, the vast caldera Sea of Sand below, and three or four other volcanic cones framing the edges. The light changes every minute between 05:15 and 06:30. Arrive before 05:00. It will be crowded, it will be cold, it will be worth every second.

Hire a jeep from the rim (don't ride the NMax up in the dark). Book jeep the day before through your guesthouse.
See photos

Mount Bromo Crater

Active stratovolcano, 2329 m. Walk across the Sea of Sand (a vast caldera floor of volcanic ash) to the base, then climb 253 concrete steps to the crater rim. At the top: a hole that drops 200 m into sulphur-yellow smoke. The Tengger people make offerings here annually during Yadnya Kasada — throwing crops, livestock, and money into the volcano.

Cross the Sea of Sand on foot (45 min) rather than by horse — the scale only makes sense walking it
See photos

Blue fire · Wild East Java

The only blue volcanic flames in the world, and Bunty turns older

08:00
Depart Probolinggo. Longest riding day: 195 km across the eastern tip of Java.
15:00
Arrive Banyuwangi. Check in. Celebrate Bunty's birthday properly. Good seafood city.
23:00
Night departure for Kawah Ijen. 2-hour drive, 3-hour hike. Blue fire only visible 23:00–05:00.
02:30
Reach the crater rim. Blue sulphur flames burning in the dark below. The world's largest acidic crater lake, pH 0.
05:30
Crater sunrise over turquoise lake. Descend. Breakfast, sleep. Afternoon: Baluran National Park — Java's savanna.

Kawah Ijen — Blue Fire

The world's only large-scale blue volcanic fire, visible only before dawn. Sulphuric gases combust as they emerge from vents, producing blue flames up to 5 m high. At daylight, the fire disappears — only the world's largest acidic crater lake (pH 0, turquoise-blue, 200 m deep) remains. The sulphur miners working the same vents at 2am make this one of the most surreal places on earth. Do not skip this. Full gas mask essential.

Blue fire is only visible 23:00–05:00. Bring: headlamp, gas mask (rent at trailhead), warm jacket, trekking poles. The hike up is 3 km, steep.
See photos

Baluran National Park

Called "Africa of Java" for its reason: open acacia savanna unlike anywhere else in the archipelago. Banteng (wild cattle), leopards, green peacocks, deer, and hundreds of bird species. The park is almost unknown internationally — usually empty. Bekol Savanna at sunrise is the standout: a flat plain under a volcanic silhouette, with banteng grazing through morning mist.

Visit Bekol Savanna at 06:00 — the park road from the entrance is also rideable on the NMax
See photos

Tanah Lot Temple

Ancient Hindu shrine perched on a rocky outcrop 300 m offshore, accessible on foot at low tide. At sunset the silhouette of the temple against an orange sky over the Indian Ocean is the defining image of Bali. Built in the 16th century by the priest Nirartha. Surrounded by sea snakes considered sacred guardians — they shelter in rock crevices below.

Arrive 1.5 hours before sunset. Walk the full coastal path around the headland for the classic angle.
See photos

Tegallalang Rice Terraces, Ubud

The UNESCO-listed subak irrigation system of Bali — rice terraces carved into valley walls over centuries using cooperative village water management. Tegallalang north of Ubud is the most photogenic: deep green stair-step valleys dropping away on both sides of a ridge road. Best at 08:00 before heat and crowds. The same system feeds over 400 km of Balinese terraces.

Hire a local guide who takes you down into the terraces — from above is just a photo. Inside is the experience.
See photos
You made it

Jakarta to Bali. 2 212 km. Fourteen stops. Three tea plantations, two active volcanoes, one world's largest Buddhist monument, one blue fire, one birthday. Every tank of fuel, every sunrise, every roadside nasi bungkus eaten by the bike — they accumulate into something that can't be explained to someone who wasn't there. That's the point of the road.

Gear list

What to bring — one bag, 20 days, one Southeast Asian October

✓
Helmet
Buy in Jakarta — better fit, cheaper than renting. Full-face recommended for highway speeds.
✓
Mesh riding jacket with armour
CE-rated shoulder and elbow inserts. Mesh panel essential — Java averages 30°C. Bring it from home.
✓
Riding gloves
Short or gauntlet style. Your hands will thank you after hour four on day one.
✓
Waterproof riding boots or shoes
October is the edge of wet season in Java. Rain comes fast. Waterproof shoes double as hiking footwear for Bromo and Ijen.
✓
Rain poncho / rain jacket
Compact packable poncho for sudden downpours. One per rider.
✓
Neck gaiter + beanie
For Bromo crater rim (cold at 2329 m) and Ijen night hike (5°C before dawn). Essential, not optional.
✓
Clothing: 8 t-shirts, 2 jeans, 2 shorts
Lightweight cotton or technical fabric. Laundry every 3–4 days available everywhere for ~50,000 IDR/kg.
✓
Gas mask for Ijen
Rentable at the Ijen trailhead for ~20,000 IDR. Do not hike without one — sulphur concentration at the crater can be lethal. Don't trust the paper masks.
✓
Power bank (20,000 mAh)
Navigation runs all day. Charge at hotels. A 20,000 mAh bank charges a phone 4–5× and runs GPS for the full road day.
✓
Phone mount + offline maps
RAM or Quad Lock mount for the NMax handlebar. Download offline maps for Java (Maps.me or Google Offline) — some rural areas have no signal.
✓
First aid + basic meds
Paracetamol, ibuprofen, rehydration salts, antiseptic cream, blister pads. Pharmacies (apotek) everywhere but saves time on the road.
✓
Indonesian SIM or eSIM
Telkomsel or XL Axiata — buy at the airport. Data is cheap (~100,000 IDR for 30 GB). Needed for Google Maps, WhatsApp navigation in convoy, weather checks.
